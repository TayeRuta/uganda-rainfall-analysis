Where Uganda's rain is actually changing

Nationally, rainfall shows no clear trend. Broken down by region, a different picture appears: the northeast is getting markedly wetter, the 2009 drought was mainly a northern and central event, and the west and the Lake Victoria basin remain the steadiest.

Key takeaways

  1. The trend is regional, not national. Karamoja (+6.7 mm/yr) and the Eastern region (+7.2 mm/yr) are getting significantly wetter. The Central, Western and Lake Victoria basin series are flat.
  2. Karamoja is drier and far less predictable. Its annual totals swing twice as much as the country's, and its short-rains totals vary by about 31% from one year to the next.
  3. Most of the wetting comes from the short rains. September–November totals rose significantly in Karamoja (p = 0.0015), Eastern and Northern. The March–May long rains show no significant trend anywhere.
  4. 2009 was a northern and central drought. It lasted 9 months in the Northern region and 7 in Central, but only 4 in Karamoja.
  5. The west and the Lake Victoria basin are steadiest, but not risk-free. Both saw severe dry spells in 2023, and the west had another in 2024.

06 · El Niño and La Niña

Even by region, El Niño years don't stand out

Part 1 suggested that national averaging might be hiding the El Niño signal. The regional run doesn't find it either: in every region, average short-rains totals in El Niño and La Niña years are within about 20 mm of each other, and no difference is significant.

07 · Limits and next steps

How far to trust these numbers

Solid

  • Karamoja's short-rains increase (p = 0.0015) is the strongest result; even across every test run here, a result this strong would turn up by chance only about 5% of the time
  • Karamoja's and Eastern's annual trends are confirmed by the rank-based Kendall test (p = 0.04 and 0.03)
  • Rankings of which region is wettest, driest and most variable
  • Where and how long each historical drought lasted

Handle with care

  • The "zones" are Uganda's four administrative regions standing in for agro-ecological zones, which aren't in Earth Engine's catalog
  • Karamoja and the Lake Victoria basin overlap the administrative regions, so the rows aren't independent
  • With about 35 tests run across the regions, one or two "significant" results at p ≈ 0.04 could be chance
  • The 2020s cover only six years, and 36 years is short for climate attribution

Recommended next steps. Swap in the official agro-ecological zone shapefile when it's available, test the Indian Ocean Dipole alongside ENSO, and combine Karamoja's rising but erratic short rains with crop calendars to see whether the extra rain arrives when it's useful.

Data table · all regional metrics

Source. CHIRPS daily precipitation (Climate Hazards Center, UC Santa Barbara), summed to monthly totals and averaged over each region in Google Earth Engine, 1990–2025.

Boundaries. Karamoja: FAO GAUL 2015 districts (Abim, Amudat, Kaabong, Kotido, Moroto, Nakapiripirit, Napak). Lake Victoria basin: HydroBASINS level 6 sub-basins upstream of the Jinja outlet, clipped to Uganda. Regions: geoBoundaries Uganda level 1. Permanent water (JRC surface water occurrence of 50% or more) is masked in every series.

Methods. Same as Part 1: linear and Kendall trend tests on annual totals, seasonal sums for March–May and September–November, and a simplified SPI-3 based on rolling three-month totals standardized by calendar month. ENSO years are approximated from NOAA ONI thresholds.
